# Main Roadmap Continuity & Route-Change Governance
**Date established:** 2026-10-11  
**Applies to:** Nivy's Business Plan Intelligence & Execution System  
**Status:** Standing project directive

## Canonical main roadmap
The single primary roadmap is:
[Business Plan Intelligence & Execution System — Implementation Roadmap](04-business-plan-intelligence-execution-implementation-roadmap-2026-10.md)

This is the authoritative direction for completing the full system—not merely the financial-model audit. Continue to use its phases, exit gates, and definition of done as the primary completion framework.

## Non-negotiable continuity rule
Do not abandon, silently replace, or lose track of the canonical roadmap. All research, implementation, experiments, tool selection, integrations, and side tasks must either advance a roadmap requirement or be explicitly recorded as a justified detour.

## Route-change protocol
When a temporary direction change or detour is useful:
1. **Record the reason** — what blocker, dependency, risk, new evidence, or efficiency gain makes the detour necessary.
2. **Map it to the main roadmap** — phase, requirement/module, and expected outcome it supports.
3. **Record the change** — what direction changed, what is being added/removed/deferred, and why.
4. **Track dependencies and impact** — effects on scope, files, time, validation, security, and other modules.
5. **Define a return point** — the exact phase/task/exit gate to resume after the detour.
6. **Close the loop** — mark the detour completed, record evidence/results, update the main tracker/register, and return to the canonical roadmap.
7. **Preserve history** — append dated decisions/change notes; do not silently rewrite history or delete source material.

## Required status discipline
Distinguish clearly between:
- Discovered / mapped
- Specified / documented
- Inspected / license-security checked
- Adapted / integrated
- Tested / independently validated
- Accepted / release-ready

A file, catalog entry, prompt, or proposed architecture is not proof of working implementation. Do not mark a capability complete without its acceptance evidence and test result. Keep assumptions, verified facts, open questions, blockers, and decisions separate.

## Scope protection
The target remains a complete, integrated, tested, usable Business Plan Intelligence & Execution System: research and evidence, strategy, sales, marketing, GTM, operations/delivery, customer success, workforce/HR, technology, finance, legal/tax and jurisdictional overlays, risk/governance, execution planning, monitoring/learning, and relevant business-type modules. Finance is one submodule, not a replacement for the overall goal.

## Standard operating loop
**Canonical roadmap → current-state check → requirement/gap mapping → reuse/adapt/integrate/build decision → implementation → tests/evidence → tracker update → return to next roadmap gate.**

Before starting a new workstream, check the roadmap and current tracker. If a missing capability is discovered, add it to the coverage register and map it to a roadmap phase instead of creating an unrelated parallel plan.

## Continuity reminder
If the user asks to continue, proceed from the next unfinished roadmap gate and the latest recorded state. Do not ask the user to re-approve the overall direction unless a material decision genuinely requires their input. Ask only for information that is essential and unavailable; otherwise continue with safe, non-destructive work.
