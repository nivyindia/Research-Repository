# International Marketing Plan Framework (Detailed)

**Type:** Framework + detailed checklist points (still not full narrative fill)  
**Level:** International / multi-country + digital  
**Sources:** SOSTAC, RACE, Keegan-style phases, market-entry logic, global–local practice, gap analysis, plan templates (Smart Insights, niBusinessInfo, international GTM guides)

**Rule:** Har section mein **kya-kya cover karna hai** points hain. Numbers, copy, final decisions = Phase 2.

---

## 0. How this framework works

### 0.1 Two planning levels (always)
- **Corporate / global:** market choice, resource commitment, brand core, governance, shared KPIs
- **Country / cluster:** local research, mix, channels, budget, local benchmarks, local owners

### 0.2 Planning cycle
- Situation → Objectives → Strategy → Programme/Tactics → Implement → Control → feedback to Situation
- Not one-time; refresh quarterly minimum for P1 markets

### 0.3 Digital layer
- Map execution to **RACE:** Plan → Reach → Act → Convert → Engage
- Every major channel must state which RACE stage it primarily serves

### 0.4 Global–local rule
- Document **what is fixed globally** vs **what local teams can adapt**
- Never assume home-market channel mix works abroad

## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 Plan identity
- Plan name / version / owner
- Time horizon (e.g. 12 months + 90-day sprint)
- Business unit / product lines in scope
- Markets in scope (list + P1/P2/P3)

### 1.2 Strategic headline
- Why international growth now
- Core international strategy in 3–5 lines
- Entry mode(s) chosen per P1 market
- Global brand posture (standardise vs adapt summary)

### 1.3 Goals and economics
- North-star business goal
- Top 3–5 marketing goals
- Headline budget (total + split media / people / tech / local)
- Expected outcomes (revenue, customers, pipeline, brand — as applicable)

### 1.4 Risks and ask
- Top 3–5 risks + mitigations
- Critical dependencies
- Decision required from leadership (approve / budget / hire / sequence)

## 2. Situation Analysis (Where are we now?)

### 2.1 Corporate situation
- Current countries / regions and revenue or volume mix
- Share of revenue from digital vs offline (if relevant)
- Brand awareness / consideration where measured
- Digital maturity (site, SEO, paid, CRM, data, automation, AI)
- Marketing maturity (process, measurement, team skills)
- Resources: budget band, headcount, agencies, tools
- Commitment level to international growth (test / invest / scale)
- Existing partners, distributors, marketplaces, affiliates
- Past international attempts and learnings

### 2.2 Macro environment — PESTEL (per P1 market / cluster)
**Political**
- Stability, trade policy, sanctions, government digital agenda
**Economic**
- GDP trend, inflation, FX, purchasing power, consumer confidence
- B2B buying cycles / public procurement if relevant
**Social / cultural**
- Demographics, language(s), values, trust norms, shopping culture
- Local holidays and peak seasons
**Technological**
- Internet / mobile / broadband penetration
- Dominant devices, payment rails, logistics tech
- Local platforms and tech stacks
**Environmental**
- Sustainability expectations, packaging/shipping norms
**Legal / regulatory**
- Consumer protection, advertising claims, promotions law
- Data privacy (GDPR-class and local equivalents)
- Sector licenses, product standards, age restrictions
- Tax, invoicing, e-invoicing requirements

### 2.3 Digital infrastructure snapshot (per market)
- Internet and smartphone penetration
- Ecommerce adoption and trust in online payment
- Dominant search engines (not always Google)
- Dominant social and messaging apps
- Dominant marketplaces / retail media
- Ad ecosystem maturity and cost benchmarks (where known)

### 2.4 Market analysis
- Category definition and boundaries
- TAM / SAM / SOM with sources and year
- Growth rate / CAGR / maturity stage
- Seasonality and demand peaks
- Price sensitivity and typical deal size
- Switching costs and loyalty patterns
- Distribution structure: DTC, retail, marketplaces, partners
- Online vs offline purchase journey weight

### 2.5 Customer analysis (4C — Customer)
- Audience universe: total, serviceable, current, lost, influencers, buyers vs users
- Segmentation bases: geographic, demographic, firmographic, psychographic, behavioral, intent, lifecycle, value
- ICP definition (ideal / high-LTV / high-intent / bad-fit)
- Personas (profile, goals, pains, triggers, objections, trust factors)
- Jobs-to-be-done (functional, emotional, social)
- Search behavior and keywords themes
- Content and social consumption habits
- Preferred platforms and devices
- Decision process and buying committee (B2B)
- LTV, frequency, churn drivers

### 2.6 Company digital audit (4C — Company)
- Website / apps: IA, UX, speed, mobile, accessibility, localisation state
- SEO: visibility, technical health, content depth, international setup (hreflang/domains)
- Content assets and gaps by language/market
- Social presence and engagement quality
- Paid media history and efficiency
- CRM / email / messaging capability and list quality
- Analytics, tracking, consent, data quality
- Conversion paths and known funnel leaks
- Brand reputation, reviews, trust signals by market
- Martech / adtech stack and integration gaps
- Automation and AI usage today
- Team skills and bandwidth by function

### 2.7 Competitor analysis (4C — Competitors)
- Direct, indirect, substitute, emerging, pure-local champions
- Per competitor: offer, pricing, positioning, proof, target segment
- Digital: site, SEO/keywords, content, social, ads, creators, CRM signals
- Strengths / weaknesses / gaps vs us
- Share of voice or visibility proxies where available
- Likely competitive responses to our entry

### 2.8 Change factors (4C — Change)
- Platform algorithm and policy shifts
- AI search / discovery shifts
- Privacy and identity changes
- Consumer behavior shifts post-crisis or generational
- Competitor moves and new entrants

### 2.9 SWOT (global + each P1 market)
**Strengths** — brand, product, cost, data, talent, partnerships, digital assets, geo advantage (with evidence)  
**Weaknesses** — awareness, conversion, content, tracking, skills, capital, localisation gaps, support coverage  
**Opportunities** — underserved segments, channel white space, competitor weakness, regulation, tech, partnerships  
**Threats** — incumbents, CAC inflation, regulation, FX, platform dependency, reputation, fraud, saturation  

### 2.10 TOWS
- **SO:** use strengths on opportunities
- **ST:** use strengths to blunt threats
- **WO:** close weaknesses to take opportunities
- **WT:** defensive moves / avoid or hedge
- Ranked strategic priorities from TOWS

### 2.11 Key issues register
For each issue:
- Issue statement
- Evidence (quant + qual)
- Business / marketing / revenue / customer impact
- Root cause hypothesis
- Urgency (H/M/L)
- Opportunity if fixed
- Recommended response direction
- KPI to watch
- Owner

### 2.12 Evidence pack standards
- Quantitative: analytics, search, ads, CRM, market reports
- Qualitative: interviews, surveys, reviews, sales notes, support tickets
- Visuals: charts, tables, competitor matrices, journey maps

## 3. Market Selection and Entry

### 3.1 Attractiveness criteria (score)
- Market size and growth
- Category profitability / willingness to pay
- Competitive intensity and concentration
- Digital readiness and CAC outlook
- Regulatory burden and time-to-compliance
- Payment / logistics feasibility
- Talent and partner availability
- Strategic spillover (regional hub value)

### 3.2 Ability-to-win criteria (score)
- Product–market fit evidence
- Brand relevance / trust path
- Existing demand signals (search, inbound, waitlist)
- Channel access (ads, SEO, partners, marketplaces)
- Operational ability (support language, SLAs, local entity needs)
- Budget and leadership attention available

### 3.3 Strategic role of market
- Beachhead / test market
- Scale / profit market
- Defensive (block competitor)
- Learning / innovation market
- Hub for regional expansion

### 3.4 Prioritization output
- P1 / P2 / P3 list with scores
- Entry sequence and rationale
- Markets explicitly **not** pursuing and why

### 3.5 Entry mode options (choose per market)
- Digital-only / export from home base
- Localised website + remote sales
- Marketplace-first
- Distributor / reseller / agency partner
- Licensing / franchise
- Joint venture / alliance
- Local subsidiary / hiring on ground
- Hybrid path and upgrade triggers (when to increase commitment)

### 3.6 Entry decision pack
- Recommended mode + why
- Investment range and payback logic
- Legal / tax / entity checklist
- 90-day validation assumptions
- Go / no-go criteria

## 4. Objectives (Where do we want to be?)

### 4.1 Corporate / business objectives
- Revenue / bookings by region
- Profit contribution or margin floor
- Customer or account counts
- Market share or category rank (if measurable)
- Strategic presence goals (e.g. live in X countries)

### 4.2 Marketing objectives (SMART)
- Brand: awareness, consideration, branded search
- Reach: traffic, impressions, share of voice proxies
- Act: engagement, leads, MQLs
- Convert: sales, CAC, conversion rates, ROAS
- Engage: retention, NRR, repeat, NPS/CSAT, advocacy

### 4.3 Objective design rules
- Global roll-up + **per P1 market** targets
- Baseline required (or explicit “unknown — measure first”)
- Time-bound and owned
- Linked to budget

### 4.4 Standard objective row
| Objective | Baseline | Target | Deadline | Market | Owner | KPI | Data source | Budget | Dependencies |

### 4.5 Pipeline / funnel math
- Revenue → deals → opportunities → SQLs → MQLs → leads → sessions (or equivalent)
- Channel contribution assumptions
- Sensitivity: best / base / worst case

### 4.6 Efficiency targets
- CAC / CPL / CPA ceilings by market
- LTV or payback targets
- Marketing % of revenue guardrails

## 5. Strategy (How do we get there?)

### 5.1 Strategic posture
- Growth model: PLG / sales-led / partner-led / hybrid (by market if different)
- Standardise vs adapt matrix:
  - Fixed globally (brand core, claims guardrails, data rules, visual system floor)
  - Flexible locally (language, proof, offers, channels, creative culture, pricing bands)
- Brand architecture: one global brand / endorsed / local brands

### 5.2 STP
**Segmentation**
- Global segment model
- Local segment variants that matter
**Targeting**
- Primary / secondary / explicit non-targets per market
**Positioning**
- Global positioning statement
- Local adaptations (proof, tone, competitors to reference)
- Message hierarchy: global core → regional → campaign

### 5.3 Value proposition and offers
- Core UVP / USP
- Benefits vs features map
- Proof types (case studies, numbers, certifications, UGC)
- Offer ladder: lead magnet → entry → core → upsell → cross-sell → retention
- Pricing and packaging local rules
- Guarantees / risk reversals if used

### 5.4 Customer journey strategy
Stages to map for each P1 market:
- Unaware → Problem-aware → Solution-aware → Brand-aware → Consideration → Intent → Purchase → Onboarding → Retention → Expansion → Advocacy  
Per stage define:
- Customer questions
- Content / asset types
- Primary channels
- CTA
- KPI
- Automation / agent opportunity

### 5.5 Channel strategy
- Priority 2–4 channels per P1 market (avoid scatter)
- Owned / paid / earned intent mix by market maturity
- Local platform map (search, social, messaging, marketplace, retail media)
- Role of each channel in RACE
- What we will **not** do in year 1 per market

### 5.6 Digital strategy (RACE detail)
**Reach:** SEO, AI search visibility, paid reach, social organic, PR, video discovery, creators  
**Act:** content engagement, site interaction, lead magnets, community  
**Convert:** landing CRO, paid conversion, sales assist, ABM, offers, checkout  
**Engage:** email/CRM, messaging apps, loyalty, lifecycle, advocacy programs  

### 5.7 Partnership and ecosystem strategy
- Affiliates, resellers, tech partners, co-marketing, marketplace strategy

### 5.8 Organisation and operating model
- Centralised / hub-spoke / decentralised / matrix
- Decision rights: brand, budget, media, creative, local offers
- Local language support and response time standards
- Agency vs in-house split by market

### 5.9 Technology and data strategy
- Shared stack vs local tools
- First-party data and consent by jurisdiction
- Tracking and identity approach
- Automation and AI use policy (high-level)

## 6. Marketing Programme / Tactics (How exactly?)

### 6.1 Product / service tactics
- Local product packaging / naming / feature flags
- Compliance labelling and claims
- Support language and hours
- Onboarding localisation
- Service recovery process

### 6.2 Price tactics
- List price, local currency display
- Tax-inclusive vs exclusive norms
- Discount / promo calendar rules
- Competitive price monitoring
- Payment terms (B2B)

### 6.3 Place / distribution tactics
- Domain / subdomain / subdirectory strategy
- Localised UX and checkout
- Marketplaces to join and ranking tactics
- Partner enablement kits
- Logistics promises and returns policy communication
- Local payment methods (cards, wallets, COD, invoice…)

### 6.4 Promotion — Brand and messaging
- Messaging house (pillars, proof, tone)
- Transcreation process (not literal translation only)
- Visual localisation rules (people, colour, symbols)
- Claim approval workflow

### 6.5 Promotion — Content
- Content pillars by market
- Formats: blog, guides, video, social, email, webinars, UGC
- Workflow: research → brief → create → local QA → publish → distribute → repurpose → measure
- SEO content vs demand-gen content split

### 6.6 Promotion — Search / SEO / AI search
- Keyword and intent research per language/market
- Technical international SEO (hreflang, locale URLs, sitemaps)
- On-page and content SEO
- Digital PR / links
- Local SEO if physical or service area
- AI search / AEO / GEO: entities, citations, structured data, brand mentions

### 6.7 Promotion — Paid media
- Paid search, paid social, display/programmatic, CTV/OTT, audio, retail/commerce media
- Campaign structure standards
- Audience and geo targeting rules
- Creative localisation and testing plan
- Budget caps, bidding, frequency
- Brand safety and fraud controls
- Tracking and conversion definitions

### 6.8 Promotion — Social and community
- Platform choices per market
- Organic calendar themes
- Community management and response SLAs
- Social commerce where relevant
- Crisis / escalation path

### 6.9 Promotion — Video and creators
- Always-on vs campaign video
- Creator discovery, vetting, contracts, disclosure
- UGC rights and usage
- Platform-native formats (Shorts/Reels/TikTok etc.)

### 6.10 Promotion — PR and reputation
- Local media list and angles
- Review generation and response
- Executive / expert visibility
- Issue management

### 6.11 Promotion — CRM, email, messaging
- Lifecycle programs (welcome, nurture, onboarding, win-back)
- Local messaging apps (WhatsApp, SMS, RCS, LINE, etc.) and opt-in law
- Preference centres and frequency caps
- Personalisation rules and data use limits

### 6.12 Promotion — Partnerships / affiliate / referral
- Partner tiers and offers
- Tracking and fraud checks
- Co-marketing calendar

### 6.13 Lead gen / demand / ABM / sales assist (if B2B or hybrid)
- Lead magnets and forms
- Scoring and routing
- ABM tiers and plays
- Sales enablement localisation
- Handoff SLAs

### 6.14 CRO / experimentation
- Priority pages and funnels per market
- Test backlog and governance
- Personalisation boundaries

### 6.15 Per-market playbook (P1 mandatory)
Each P1 market file/section includes:
- Local ICP / persona notes
- Offer and pricing notes
- Channel priority list
- 90-day calendar outline
- KPI set and owners
- Local risks and compliance notes
- Budget envelope

## 7. Implementation / Action Plan

### 7.1 Initiative backlog structure
| Priority | Initiative | Market | RACE stage | Owner | Start | End | Budget | Dependency | KPI | Status |

### 7.2 Time horizons
- Days 0–30: foundations (tracking, legal, localisation basics, pilot campaigns)
- Days 31–60: scale tests, content engine, partner onboarding
- Days 61–90: double-down winners, cut losers, first optimization cycle
- Months 4–6 / 7–12: scale and systemise

### 7.3 Workstreams (typical)
- Legal / entity / privacy
- Site and localisation
- Measurement and data
- SEO and content
- Paid media
- CRM and lifecycle
- Partnerships
- Creative production
- Hiring / agency onboarding

### 7.4 Resources
- Internal roles and % allocation
- Agencies and scopes
- Tools licenses by market
- Local freelancers / creators budget
- RACI for each workstream

### 7.5 Budget model
- Total by market
- Split: media / creative / tools / people / contingency (10–15%)
- Test vs scale pools
- Reallocation rules (when to shift spend across markets/channels)

### 7.6 Risk and dependency register
- Legal blockers
- Payment and tax setup
- Hiring lag
- Creative bottlenecks
- Platform account approvals
- FX and cost inflation
- Mitigation owner + date

### 7.7 Launch checklist (per market)
- Privacy notices and consent live
- Tracking validated
- Localised key pages live
- Support path live
- Ad accounts and pixels ready
- KPI dashboard live
- Owner named for first 30 days

## 8. Control and Measurement

### 8.1 KPI hierarchy
1. Business outcomes (revenue, margin, customers)
2. Marketing outcomes (pipeline, CAC, LTV, retention)
3. Market-level outcomes (same metrics with **local benchmarks**)
4. Channel KPIs (SEO, paid, social, email…)
5. Campaign / asset KPIs
6. Process KPIs (speed to publish, experiment velocity)

### 8.2 Metric dictionary
- Shared definitions across countries (lead, MQL, conversion, active user…)
- Local benchmark notes (what “good” means in DE vs IN vs US etc.)

### 8.3 Measurement stack
- Web / app analytics
- Search Console (or local equivalent)
- Ad platforms
- CRM / product analytics
- Social analytics
- Attribution model (and limits)
- Incrementality / MMM if used

### 8.4 Dashboards
- Global roll-up
- Per P1 market view
- Channel deep-dives
- Budget vs actual

### 8.5 Cadence
- **Weekly:** spend, leading indicators, incidents
- **Monthly:** market performance, channel mix, test results
- **Quarterly:** strategy, market tier changes, budget reallocation
- **Annual:** full plan refresh and Situation update

### 8.6 Optimization loop
1. Measure  
2. Diagnose (market vs channel vs offer vs creative vs tracking)  
3. Hypothesis  
4. Experiment  
5. Result  
6. Learn  
7. Update playbook / SOP / agent  
8. Scale or stop  

### 8.7 Governance and compliance
- Brand approval path
- Legal / claims approval
- Privacy and data retention
- AI content and agent usage rules
- Vendor and creator contracts
- Brand safety / fraud monitoring
- Escalation matrix

### 8.8 Audit triggers
- CAC above ceiling X weeks
- Tracking break
- Compliance complaint
- Major platform policy change
- New competitor shock
