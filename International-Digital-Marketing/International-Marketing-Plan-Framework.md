# International Marketing Plan Framework

**Type:** Structure only (framework)  
**Level:** International / multi-country  
**Sources synthesized:** SOSTAC (PR Smith); international marketing planning steps (situation → strategy → mix → implement → control); Keegan-style phases; market-entry logic (attractiveness × capability × entry mode); corporate vs country-level planning; global–local adaptation; RACE for digital channels; common “must-have” sections from global plan guides.

**Rule:** Yeh complete skeleton hai. Har box mein baad mein data/strategy fill hogi — abhi sirf proper framework.

---

## 0. How this framework works

International plans run on **two levels at once**:

| Level | Focus |
|-------|--------|
| **Corporate / global** | Which markets, resource commitment, brand core, governance |
| **Country / cluster** | Local research, mix, channels, budget, KPIs |

Planning is **iterative**: Situation → Objectives → Strategy → Programme → Implement → Control → feedback into Situation.

Digital execution maps to **RACE**: Plan → Reach → Act → Convert → Engage.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- Purpose of the plan and time horizon
- Markets in scope (P1 / P2 / P3)
- Business and marketing goals (headline)
- Core international strategy (one paragraph)
- Entry mode(s) chosen
- Headline budget and expected outcomes
- Top risks and mitigations
- Decision required from leadership

---

## 2. Situation Analysis (Where are we now?)

### 2.1 Corporate situation
- Current markets and revenue mix
- Brand strength and digital maturity
- Resources, capabilities, commitment level to international growth
- Existing partnerships and distribution

### 2.2 Macro environment (per priority market or cluster)
- PESTEL: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal
- Trade, tariffs, data/privacy, advertising and consumer law
- Digital infrastructure (internet, mobile, payments, logistics)

### 2.3 Market analysis
- Market size: TAM / SAM / SOM
- Growth, maturity, seasonality
- Customer behavior and cultural context
- Distribution and channel structure (online + offline)
- Local and global competitors

### 2.4 Internal analysis
- Strengths and weaknesses vs local and global rivals
- Product/service fit for market
- Ability to support local marketing, sales, service

### 2.5 SWOT (global + per priority market)
- Strengths | Weaknesses | Opportunities | Threats
- Evidence required (quant + qual)

### 2.6 TOWS (optional but recommended)
- SO / ST / WO / WT strategic options

### 2.7 Key issues and opportunities
- Ranked list: issue → impact → response direction

---

## 3. Market Selection and Entry

### 3.1 Screening criteria
- Attractiveness: size, growth, profitability, risk, digital readiness
- Fit: capability, brand relevance, operational readiness
- Strategic role: beachhead, scale market, defensive, learning market

### 3.2 Market prioritization
- Tier P1 / P2 / P3
- Scorecard (attractiveness × ability to win)
- Sequence of entry

### 3.3 Entry mode (per market)
- Export / digital-only
- Licensing / franchising
- Distributor / partner
- Joint venture / alliance
- Own subsidiary / local entity
- Rationale and commitment level

### 3.4 Go / no-go and assumptions
- Critical assumptions to validate in first 90 days

---

## 4. Objectives (Where do we want to be?)

### 4.1 Corporate objectives
- Revenue, profit, share, brand, strategic presence by region

### 4.2 Marketing objectives (SMART)
- Awareness, consideration, traffic, leads, sales, retention — global and by market

### 4.3 Objective format
| Objective | Baseline | Target | Deadline | Market | Owner | KPI | Budget |

### 4.4 Forecast link
- Sales and pipeline forecast assumptions by market

---

## 5. Strategy (How do we get there?)

### 5.1 Strategic posture
- Global standardisation vs local adaptation (what is fixed vs flexible)
- Brand architecture (global brand / local brand / dual)

### 5.2 STP (global + local)
- **Segmentation:** geo, cultural, behavioral, value, firmographic
- **Targeting:** primary segments per market
- **Positioning:** global core message + local proof and tone

### 5.3 Value proposition and offer
- Core value proposition
- Local offer, pricing, packaging adaptations
- Competitive differentiation per market

### 5.4 Customer journey (international)
- Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Onboarding → Retention → Advocacy
- Local differences in discovery and trust

### 5.5 Channel strategy
- Priority channels per market (not copy-paste of home market)
- Owned / paid / earned mix
- Local platforms (e.g. regional social, marketplaces, messaging apps)

### 5.6 Digital strategy layer (RACE)
- **Reach:** SEO, paid, social, PR, video, creators
- **Act:** engagement, content, leads
- **Convert:** CRO, sales, offers
- **Engage:** CRM, lifecycle, loyalty

### 5.7 Organisation model
- Centralised / decentralised / hub-spoke / matrix
- Local vs global decision rights (brand, budget, creative, media)

---

## 6. Marketing Programme / Tactics (How exactly?)

### 6.1 Product / service
- Fit, localisation, compliance, support language

### 6.2 Price
- Currency, tax, willingness-to-pay, competitive price bands, promotions rules

### 6.3 Place / distribution
- Website, marketplaces, partners, logistics, payment methods

### 6.4 Promotion (integrated)
- Brand and messaging framework (non-negotiables)
- Content and localisation / transcreation
- SEO and international search
- Paid media (search, social, display, retail media, etc.)
- Social and community
- Video and creators
- PR and reputation
- Email / CRM / messaging
- Partnerships and affiliates
- Events / offline where relevant

### 6.5 People / process / physical evidence (services)
- Local service standards, SLAs, trust signals

### 6.6 Per-market playbook outline
- One short playbook structure per P1 market: audience, offer, channels, calendar, KPI, owner

---

## 7. Implementation / Action Plan

### 7.1 Roadmap
| Priority | Initiative | Market | Owner | Start | End | Budget | KPI | Status |

### 7.2 Horizons
- 30 / 60 / 90 days
- 6 months / 12 months

### 7.3 Resources
- Team, agencies, tools, local partners
- RACI for major workstreams

### 7.4 Budget
- By market and by channel
- Media vs production vs people vs tech
- Test budget vs scale budget

### 7.5 Dependencies and risks
- Legal, payment, logistics, hiring, creative, tech launch blockers

---

## 8. Control and Measurement

### 8.1 KPI hierarchy
- Business KPIs
- Marketing KPIs (global)
- Market-level KPIs (local benchmarks)
- Channel and campaign KPIs

### 8.2 Measurement system
- Analytics, CRM, ad platforms, search consoles
- Attribution approach
- Shared definitions across countries

### 8.3 Reporting cadence
- Weekly (tactical)
- Monthly (performance by market)
- Quarterly (strategy and reallocation)
- Annual (plan refresh)

### 8.4 Optimization loop
- Measure → Diagnose → Test → Learn → Standardise or localise further → Scale

### 8.5 Governance
- Brand, legal, privacy, AI, vendor approvals
- Escalation paths
