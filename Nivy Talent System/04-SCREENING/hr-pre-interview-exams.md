## Typical Selection Funnel for an HR Profile

A robust process usually has 4–5 rounds, each probing a different competency crucial for HR success.

### Round 1: Application & Resume Screening

- **Purpose:** Filter candidates who meet basic qualifications and foundational knowledge.
- **Format:** Resume and application form review.
- **Assessed:**
  - Educational background (HR, Psychology, Business Administration, or related)
  - Skills: HRIS (SAP, Oracle), MS Office, ATS experience
  - Internships / prior HR or admin experience
  - Achievements indicating interpersonal and organizational skills
  - Attention to detail (resume quality)

### Round 2: HR-Specific Aptitude & Knowledge Test

- **Purpose:** Objectively evaluate core HR knowledge, cognitive ability, situational judgment.
- **Format:** Online timed test (60–75 minutes).
- **Assessed:**
  - Core HR knowledge (MCQs): labour laws (minimum wage, PF, ESI, gratuity, POSH), HR cycles, terminology (CTC vs Gross vs Net, attrition, engagement)
  - Verbal & written ability: grammar, email drafting
  - Logical reasoning & data interpretation: attrition, attendance, recruitment metrics
  - Situational Judgment Tests (SJTs): realistic HR dilemmas

### Round 3: Operational / Technical Interview

- **Purpose:** Practical, process-oriented knowledge of HR functions.
- **Format:** Video or in-person with HR Manager / Senior HR Generalist.
- **Assessed:** End-to-end recruitment process, HRIS/Excel proficiency, compliance (onboarding docs, POSH), scenario questions, confidentiality handling.

### Round 4: Behavioral & Cultural Fit Interview

- **Purpose:** Soft skills, ethics, empathy, culture alignment.
- **Format:** Interview with Head of HR or senior leader; STAR method.
- **Assessed:** Conflict resolution, ethics, empathy, employee-facing service mindset, motivation for HR.

### Round 5: Final HR Case Study / Practical Assignment

- **Purpose:** Strategic thinking and practical application.
- **Examples:** Attrition analysis + action plan; draft Work from Home policy; recruitment strategy for niche roles; rewrite a flawed JD.

### Ideal profile: the 3 H's

1. **Head** — Analytical & legal: laws, processes, data; precise and compliant.
2. **Heart** — Empathy & ethics: fair, discreet, culture-building.
3. **Hands** — Execution & communication: offer letters, conflict de-escalation, strong operations.

## Sample Question Bank (from source)

### Section 1A — Core HR Knowledge (sample)

1. Correct sequence of stages in the recruitment lifecycle?  
   a) Sourcing → Screening → Interviewing → Onboarding → Offer  
   b) Sourcing → Screening → Interviewing → Offer → Onboarding  
   c) Screening → Sourcing → Interviewing → Offer → Onboarding  
   d) Onboarding → Sourcing → Screening → Interviewing → Offer  
   **Preferred:** (b)

2. Primary purpose of a Job Description (JD)?  
   → Communicate key responsibilities, qualifications, and reporting structure.

3. Most critical compliance activity during onboarding?  
   → Collection of documents for PF, ESI, and background verification.

4. Offer letter should include all EXCEPT: future bonus predictions.

5. Main goal of exit formalities?  
   → Understand reason for departure and ensure smooth handover of responsibilities and assets.

6–10. Cover required first-day documents, onboarding impact, sourcing definition, screening purpose, Full & Final Settlement components.

### Section 1B — Compliance & Laws (sample themes)

- POSH: ICC required for organizations with more than **10** employees; ICC chaired by a **senior female employee** (Presiding Officer).
- EPF employee contribution rate: **12%** of basic.
- Gratuity: after **5 years** continuous service.
- Minimum wages: set by respective **State Governments**.
- ESI: contribution for employees whose gross salary is up to **₹21,000**/month.
- Failure to pay minimum wages: legal offense (fines/imprisonment).
- ESI benefits: medical and cash benefits for sickness, maternity, employment injury.

### Section 2 — Communication & Scenarios (sample themes)

- Grammar (subject-verb agreement, apostrophes, professional closings).
- Email subject lines: specific and actionable.
- Rejection tone: professional, respectful, courteous.
- Scenarios: calm private de-escalation of office arguments; negotiate within budget when candidate has competing offer; confidentiality of salary data; document exit-interview allegations and investigate patterns; proactively fix payroll underpayments; constructive rejection feedback; background discrepancy → contact candidate for clarification; mediate inter-department conflicts neutrally.

### Section 3 — Data / Logical (sample themes)

- Attendance & overtime tables; attrition charts; basic reasoning sequences.
