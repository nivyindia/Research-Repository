# Objection Handling — Institute Growth & Industry Partnership Program

**Tone:** Calm, respectful, factual. Never argumentative.  
**Goal:** Understand the real concern, answer honestly, and keep the door open for a limited pilot conversation.

---

### 1. “Placement people keep coming. How are you different?”

**Response:**  
I understand the fatigue, Sir. Most placement drives only arrive at the end and focus on interviews. We start much earlier — with practical training design, repeated workflows, assessment and portfolio — so that when an opportunity does appear, the student is actually ready. We also never promise placement numbers. Difference is process first, opportunity second.

---

### 2. “If the pilot is free, you will lock us in later.”

**Response:**  
That is exactly why we freeze a written annex before anything starts. Only the items listed there are included. After six months there is a clean review point — continue, expand, or stop. No automatic renewal and no penalty clause for ending. The free scope is limited on purpose so both sides stay honest.

---

### 3. “We already have good teachers / our own courses.”

**Response:**  
We are not asking to replace your teachers or your existing courses. Many partners keep their faculty and simply add structured practical task design, better assessment and career-preparation modules on top. Your strength stays; we add the industry operating layer.

---

### 4. “Students only want government certificates / CCC / basic courses.”

**Response:**  
Those courses still have demand and we do not ask you to drop them. The pilot sits alongside, for students who want deeper practical exposure in Accounting operations or Digital Marketing. It is an addition, not a replacement of your current portfolio.

---

### 5. “How do we know your training quality is real?”

**Response:**  
That is why we insist on a limited pilot with written scope and monthly visibility. You will see the materials, the tasks, the assessment approach and the student output. Quality is proven in the first cohorts, not claimed in a brochure.

---

### 6. “We don’t have time / staff to coordinate another program.”

**Response:**  
Coordination load is discussed and written into the pilot annex. We design for limited cohorts precisely so the extra load stays manageable. If your current capacity is tight, we can start even smaller or adjust the delivery split. We will not push a model that overloads your team.

---

### 7. “What if students complain or results are weak?”

**Response:**  
Then the pilot review will show it clearly. We prefer early visibility of problems over long hidden failure. The six-month structure exists so both sides can stop or correct course with evidence, not with blame.

---

### 8. “You will take our best students / poach them.”

**Response:**  
Any opportunity matching happens with transparency and only where a real opening exists. We do not run parallel secret recruitment against the institute. The partnership is designed so successful students reflect well on both sides.

---

### 9. “Give us the commercial numbers first.”

**Response:**  
Commercial terms for the post-pilot phase are not frozen yet and I will not invent percentages. What we can freeze immediately is the pilot scope — what is included, what is not, and how we measure success. Once both sides see the operating reality, commercial discussion becomes grounded instead of speculative.

---

### 10. “We need to discuss with partners / family / other decision makers.”

**Response:**  
Of course. I can leave the one-page summary and the pilot overview. When the relevant people are available, I am happy to walk through the same points with them or answer questions by call. No pressure on timeline.

---

### 11. “We have been cheated by training / franchise companies before.”

**Response:**  
I have heard this story from several institutes. That is why we refuse long exclusive contracts at the start and why every free element must be written down in advance. You keep control of your brand and your existing revenue. The pilot is deliberately small and time-bound so trust is built on evidence, not on promises.

---

### 12. Silence or “We’ll think about it”

**Response:**  
Understood. May I leave the one-pager and check back in [X days]? If any specific concern comes up in the meantime, you can message me directly and I will answer plainly.

---

## General Principles for Handling Objections

1. Listen fully before answering.  
2. Acknowledge the legitimacy of the concern.  
3. Answer with the actual model (pilot, written scope, no job guarantee).  
4. Offer a low-risk next step rather than pushing for immediate commitment.  
5. Never invent commercial terms to close a conversation.
