# Partner Welcome Kit — Long-Format Master Guide
**Audience:** Franchisee / Retailer  
**Status:** Structured working draft; use approved source documents to complete facts.

## 1. Welcome and company profile
Insert approved company description, service footprint, credentials, legal identity and support contacts. Do not imply licenses, authorizations or affiliations without evidence.

## 2. Partner business model
Document the partner's activities, the company's activities, service delivery ownership, customer communication responsibilities, territory/ exclusivity if any, and escalation boundaries. **[VERIFY AGREEMENT]**

## 3. Commercial plan
Record franchise/onboarding fee, deposits if applicable, recurring charges, renewals, cancellation/termination and any other commercial terms exactly as approved. If a term is not established, mark it pending; do not infer it.

## 4. Eligibility, KYC and onboarding
Provide eligibility criteria, application steps, document checklist, verification process, agreement execution, training and activation checklist. Store blank forms here; do not store actual customer/partner identity documents in the public repository.

## 5. Complete services catalogue
For each service record: service name, customer need, target segment, scope, exclusions, documents, responsible processing team, estimated service timeline (only if verified), customer price source and escalation path.

## 6. Pricing and commission
Link to `../../03_Shared-Master-References/PRICING-COMMISSION-MATRIX-TEMPLATE.md` until an approved master source is identified. Maintain effective date and approver. Keep statutory/government fees, third-party costs, company fee and partner share distinct.

## 7. Earnings and business planning
Provide a calculator and scenarios based on verified margins and clearly stated assumptions. Include low/base/high order-volume scenarios, operating costs and net contribution. Label all outputs as illustrative, not guaranteed.

## 8. Sales and customer acquisition
Define customer segments, prospecting methods, discovery questions, approved service pitches, quote process, follow-up cadence and lead logging. Avoid misleading claims or unauthorized guarantees.

## 9. Marketing and online support
List each support item, owner, eligibility, service level, partner inputs, cost (if any), and exclusions. Separate confirmed company commitments from proposed support. Specify how online leads are assigned, accepted, tracked and returned if not actioned.

## 10. Customer service and operations SOP
Cover enquiry, qualification, consent, quote, document collection, quality check, payment, order submission, acknowledgement, status updates, delivery, feedback, complaints, refunds and escalation.

## 11. Payment and settlement
Document authorized collection methods, receipts, settlement schedule, commission eligibility, reversals, refunds, disputes and reconciliation. Never ask a partner to use unapproved personal accounts.

## 12. Brand, privacy and conduct
Use only approved logos and creatives. Do not alter official pricing, promise approvals, misrepresent government affiliation, share customer data without authorization or use documents for unrelated purposes.

## 13. Training and knowledge checks
Provide onboarding modules, service knowledge, sales practice, system training, customer data handling, assessment and refresher process. Link existing Training Folder materials rather than duplicating them.

## 14. Reporting and performance
Track enquiries, qualified leads, orders, conversion, pending cases, service quality, complaints, revenue/commission and training completion. Define reporting frequency and owner.

## 15. FAQ and reference library
Include approved answers to frequent questions and links to master service catalogue, pricing, forms, SOPs, support directory and current marketing assets.

## 16. First 30 days launch plan
Week 1: onboarding, training and tools. Week 2: local prospect list and initial outreach. Week 3: follow-ups and first orders. Week 4: review conversion, service quality and support gaps. Set numeric targets only after management approves realistic benchmarks.

## Approval gates
Commercial owner: prices/fees/commission. Operations owner: service scope/SOP. Marketing owner: lead and marketing commitments. Legal/compliance reviewer: agreements, privacy and regulatory statements. Brand owner: identity and collateral.
