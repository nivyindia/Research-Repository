# ICP, Market Prioritization & 400-Prospect Research Framework

## Objective
Build a qualified list of 400 target accounts for outbound sales, while using the same ICP evidence to decide which service pages and keyword clusters deserve priority. The 400 accounts are a sales-prospect list, not a search-volume target.

## ICP hypotheses to validate
| ICP segment | Trigger / pain signal | Relevant offer | Qualification evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B professional-service firms | Website exists but service pages are weak, inconsistent content, low organic visibility | SEO + GEO/AEO | Clear service niche, identifiable owner/marketing lead, active client acquisition |
| Recruitment and staffing firms | Competitive niche, frequent hiring/sector content, weak lead conversion | B2B SEO + content + AI visibility | Sector specialization, live vacancies or active client development |
| Specialist digital/IT agencies | Need more qualified leads, service pages lack differentiation, may need white-label support | SEO/GEO execution or white-label delivery | Defined service portfolio, evidence of client work, partner/contact route |
| Growth-stage B2B SaaS | Strong product but weak category/comparison coverage | Technical/content SEO + GEO | Product-market signal, pricing/demo path, team and content capacity |
| Selected e-commerce businesses | Category/product SEO issues and measurable organic revenue opportunity | E-commerce SEO | Product-market fit, adequate catalog and analytics readiness |

## Disqualification signals
- No clear offer or no functioning business presence.
- Wants guaranteed #1 rankings or guaranteed AI citations.
- No access to implement fixes and no willingness to fund implementation.
- Very low budget relative to scope, or no decision-maker path.
- Portfolio/claims requested that Nivy Next cannot honestly substantiate.

## Geographic planning
Use country as a field, not a substitute for ICP:
- United States
- United Kingdom
- Canada / broader North America
- Australia
- New Zealand
- Europe, broken down by individual country (initial research candidates: Ireland, Netherlands and selected English-compatible segments; confirm language and demand before outreach)
Do not assume equal allocation. Start with a balanced pilot, then allocate the remaining accounts based on response, qualification and sales-conversion evidence.

## 400-account sampling plan (initial, adjustable)
| Market group | Initial account quota | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| United States | 160 | Largest initial test cell; segment by ICP, not just geography |
| United Kingdom | 70 | Test UK spelling and local competitor landscape |
| Canada / North America outside US | 50 | Keep Canada separate from US in reporting |
| Australia | 45 | Test service demand, time-zone fit and local competitors |
| New Zealand | 20 | Smaller exploratory cell |
| Selected Europe | 55 | Split by country and language; do not label all as one market |
| **Total** | **400** | Reallocate after the first outreach/qualification cohort |

These are planning quotas, not claims about market size. Adjust using real response and close-rate data.

## Required fields for every account
1. Company name and canonical domain
2. Country, region, language and time zone
3. Industry / ICP segment
4. Company size band and evidence/source
5. Decision-maker name/title and public professional profile
6. Business email or contact route with source and date
7. Website/service-page URL
8. Observable SEO/AI visibility opportunity (specific, non-speculative)
9. Trigger signal (new service, hiring, expansion, outdated content, competitor pressure)
10. Relevant Nivy Next offer and tailored reason to contact
11. Competitor(s) they appear to compete with
12. Qualification status, outreach status, last contact, next action
13. Source URL, collection date, confidence and lawful-use/compliance notes

## Account research process
1. Define one ICP segment per batch.
2. Identify companies through legitimate public sources, professional directories, industry associations, company sites and business databases.
3. Verify the company is active and the decision-maker is relevant.
4. Manually inspect the company's public service pages and content; note specific opportunities rather than making unsupported claims.
5. Check the firm's search/AI visibility with a consistent, documented test set if feasible.
6. Score fit and intent; do not confuse a weak website with willingness to buy.
7. Personalize outreach based on a verifiable observation.
8. Record outcome and feed objections/wins back into website copy and keyword priorities.

## Scoring model (1–5 each)
- ICP fit (25%)
- Visible need / trigger (25%)
- Ability and likely willingness to pay (20%)
- Access to decision-maker (15%)
- Service delivery fit / proof readiness (15%)

A score is a prioritization aid, not a factual claim. Keep evidence next to each score.

## Data quality, privacy and outreach compliance
- Use business-relevant public contact information and reputable sources.
- Respect platform terms, opt-outs, privacy law and applicable email/marketing rules in each market (including UK/EU rules and Canada's CASL where applicable).
- Do not scrape or enrich personal data in ways that violate platform terms or law.
- Store source, collection date, lawful-use rationale where required, and opt-out/suppression status.
- Do not claim a prospect was “interested” until there is evidence of engagement. “Target account” and “interested lead” are different statuses.

## Feedback loop to SEO/GEO
For every qualified or lost opportunity, record:
- What they searched for or how they described the problem
- Which competitor they considered
- Which proof or objection mattered
- Which service and price point they considered
- Whether website content answered their question
Use these signals to update keyword map, FAQs, comparison pages, offers and ICP priorities.
